# M02.07 cost calibration: priced envelopes per profile and workload

`provisional`. Generated by `python3 docs/plan/tools/cost_calibration.py` from [cost-calibration-inputs.json](../cost-calibration-inputs.json); tests in `tools/tests/test_cost_calibration.py` (hand-built traces with exact expected counts, each seen red under a deliberate mutation). It prices **counted object requests, KMS wraps and directed cross-AZ payload bytes** only. It omits compute, storage, NAT/endpoints, discounts and free tier, the journal profiles' volumes and compute and network framing, reads outside recovery, retries beyond the measured hot-key factors, and any latency or availability prediction. The rate card is the 2026-11-01 illustrative example set from [cost-economics](../research/cost-economics.md): **not a regional quote** (the tool prints `NOT A QUOTE` unless the card declares `operator_quote`). The measured inputs are the provisional sandbox numbers in [m02-bench-profile-bulk.md](m02-bench-profile-bulk.md). An estimate is never an actual bill; M15 reconciles real usage.

## What was built

- A versioned rate-card schema: every price carries value, unit, source URL and date, and a card must declare a known `status`; anything else is rejected. A price change is a data edit (tested for every row); an unpriced request kind raises instead of costing zero.
- Workloads are parameterised (streams, mean rate, record bytes, hot share, optional on/off bursts) and the trace is generated from those parameters, so editing the inputs changes the simulation. Sparse, busy and skewed match the M00 shapes; `bursty` has the same mean rate as busy but arrives in 1 s bursts every 10 s; `recovery` is one cell loss with every domain at its replay bound.
- Four profiles. Object profiles (`object-economy` 1 s wait, `object-resilient` 250 ms) commit one fenced-append PUT per batch plus amortized checkpoint PUTs (2 per 64 object writes, amortized per write, not truncated per 30 s trace) and one KMS wrap per batch. Journal profiles (`latency-ha`: three zones, two cross-AZ copies; `latency-zonal`: one zone) commit to the quorum journal, so only the asynchronous 5 s archive batches (and their checkpoints) reach the object store; the journal's volumes and compute are **not priced**, so the object-request cost of the journal profiles is not a total cost comparable with the object profiles.

`dirty domains` is the count of distinct tenant/key domains that wrote in the 30 s window. `p95 batch wait` is the batching-wait floor only, **not a p99 or acknowledgement latency**. Object PUT latency adds the measured sandbox p50 of about 12 ms at 64 KiB, 22 ms at 1 MiB and 46 ms at 4 MiB, plus KMS (M05.08, unmeasured) and the journal fsync (not measured). `min write processes` divides PUT/s by the measured small-PUT saturation of one sandbox client (about 710/s).

Readings, each recomputed from the table:
- Batching wait is the main cost dial for object profiles: busy at 1 s costs 26% of busy at 250 ms (USD 846 against 3,294 per 30 days) for a 958 ms against 245 ms wait floor. Sparse load saves only 15% because its batches are small anyway.
- KMS wraps are about 37% of object-profile cost at these rates (one per batch, 0.003 per 1000 against 0.005 per 1000 for a PUT). Inline wrapped keys save a PUT, not a KMS request. The share is much higher for the journal profiles (their object PUTs are archive only, so KMS is most of what is priced there). M05.08 must measure the real KMS path before the envelopes are frozen.
- `latency-ha` against `latency-zonal` on busy load differs by USD 495 per 30 days, exactly the cross-AZ bytes (2 copies of the raw payload, no framing or client hop) at the example 0.02 per GB. The journal profiles' request cost is lower than the object profiles' only because commits are not object PUTs; that is not a statement that they are cheaper overall.
- Skewed load is cheaper than busy under the fenced-append design (fewer, fuller batches on the hot tenant) because there is no head CAS. The measured hot-key amplification (about 1.29 PUT, 1.38 HEAD and 0.29 LIST per commit on the hot domain, M02.10) applies only to a payload-plus-head-CAS design with competing writers, a further reason that design is not the default.

## Recovery event (one cell loss)

Requests per event for 1000 domains: {'get': 66000, 'list': 1000, 'head': 0, 'put': 2000, 'kms': 1000} (reactivation PUTs, checkpoint and tail GETs, one bounded cold LIST per domain, KMS unwraps), about USD 0.04 per event. Request price is negligible; the open question is time. **No GET throughput was measured** (only PUT throughput, with 120 MiB/s as a lower bound on that rate), so read time is shown as a sensitivity at assumed single-process rates, not as a floor or bound:
- Tail at the replay-bound cap (every domain at 64 batches of 4 MiB): 250.0 GiB; 50 MiB/s: 5120 s, 120 MiB/s: 2133 s, 500 MiB/s: 512 s.
- Tail at the typical busy batch size (about 32 KiB per batch, same 64-batch bound): 2.0 GiB; 50 MiB/s: 40 s, 120 MiB/s: 17 s, 500 MiB/s: 4 s.
So the tail size actually reached, set by the replay bound and batch size, decides the RTO more than any request price; it must be measured in M03/M06 with real reads. This is a hand-set single event, not a trace-driven recovery workload.

## Assumption checks

- Zero idle: an empty trace through every profile prices to USD 0 (no polling term exists).
- Poisson/window formula (batches per domain per second = rate / (1 + rate x wait)) against the simulation:
  - sparse at 0.25 s: simulated 94.58, formula 94.12, difference 0.5%
  - busy at 0.25 s: simulated 155.82, formula 155.04, difference 0.5%
  - busy at 1.0 s: simulated 40.0, formula 39.68, difference 0.8%
  - bursty at 0.25 s: simulated 16.0, formula 155.04, difference 89.7%
  - bursty at 1.0 s: simulated 4.0, formula 39.68, difference 89.9%
  The formula holds for Poisson arrivals, but those are the same generator the formula assumes, so that agreement checks the batcher, not the real world. On the same mean rate in 10%-duty bursts the formula overstates batches roughly tenfold, so **it is an upper bound on request count for bursty traffic, not an estimate**; a real trace is needed before any envelope is frozen.
- Rejected, each tested: a price without source/date/unit, a negative or non-numeric price, an unknown card status, an unpriced request kind.

## Constraints per profile

| profile | measurable constraint frozen as an input (operator may change the value, not the semantics) | not established here |
|---|---|---|
| `object-economy` | max batch wait 1 s; one ordered lane per domain; regional object commit; checkpoint 2 PUTs per 64 writes | cell reactivation time (RTO), KMS latency (M05.08) |
| `object-resilient` | max batch wait 250 ms; same commit path; N-1 serving capacity declared by the operator | surviving-capacity and cache-duplication cost, read-path spend |
| `latency-ha` | three full voters in three zones, two cross-AZ copies, 5 s archive window (the RPO exposure bound as declared, not measured) | journal fsync latency and operations (no prototype), archive lag under failure, journal volume and compute cost |
| `latency-zonal` | one-zone journal, 5 s archive window, explicit loss exposure on zone loss | same as above |

No universal USD budget or SLA is set.

## Not done (so M02.07 stays open)

Dirty-domain counts are reported, but **no query/freshness-choice comparison** (`published`/`at_least`/`latest` barrier and tail-read spend), **no p99 or capacity report beyond the batching-wait floor and the single-process PUT saturation**, no trace-driven recovery workload, no measured RPO/RTO, real provider prices (the card is illustrative; its cross-AZ source is a load-balancer blog post, weak provenance), compute and warm-cache cost, and actual-bill reconciliation (M15).
