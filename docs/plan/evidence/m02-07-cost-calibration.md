# M02.07 cost calibration: priced envelopes per profile and workload

`provisional`. Generated by `python3 docs/plan/tools/cost_calibration.py` from [cost-calibration-inputs.json](../cost-calibration-inputs.json); tests in `tools/tests/test_cost_calibration.py`. It prices **counted requests and directed bytes** only: no compute, storage, NAT, retries, reads outside recovery, billing discounts or free tier, and no latency or availability prediction. The rate card is the 2026-11-01 illustrative example set from [cost-economics](../research/cost-economics.md), **not a regional quote**; the measured inputs are the provisional sandbox numbers in [m02-bench-profile-bulk.md](m02-bench-profile-bulk.md). An estimate here is never an actual bill; M15 reconciles real usage.

## What was built

- A versioned rate-card schema where **every price carries value, unit, source URL and date** and is rejected otherwise. A price change is a data edit (a test changes PUT from 0.005 to 0.0025 per 1000 and the cost falls); an unpriced request kind raises instead of costing zero.
- Four workloads: sparse, busy and skewed reuse the seeded 30 s traces from the M00 batching experiments (3 seeds, 1 KiB records, 4 MiB cap, tenant/key domains); recovery is one cell loss with every domain at its replay bound.
- Four profiles (`object-economy` 1 s wait, `object-resilient` 250 ms, `latency-ha` three-zone journal with two cross-AZ copies and a 5 s regional archive, `latency-zonal` single-zone journal with the same archive). Commit path for the object profiles is the fenced ordered append (one PUT per batch plus an amortized checkpoint, one KMS wrap per batch); the journal profiles add one archive PUT per archive window.

`p95 batch wait` is the batching-wait floor only (cap minus coalescing), **not a p99 or acknowledgement latency**: object PUT latency adds the measured p50 of about 12 ms at 64 KiB, 22 ms at 1 MiB and 46 ms at 4 MiB on the sandbox, the KMS path (M05.08, unmeasured) and the quorum fsync for the journal profiles (not measured). `min write processes` divides PUT/s by the measured small-PUT saturation of one sandbox client (about 710/s); every profile fits one process at these rates, so capacity is not the constraint here, cost and the wait are.

Readings:
- Longer batching is the main dial: busy load at 1 s costs about a quarter of 250 ms (829 vs 3,266 USD per 30 days) for a 958 ms vs 245 ms wait floor. Sparse load barely benefits (1,673 vs 1,961) because its batches are small anyway.
- KMS wraps are about 37% of the object-profile cost at these rates (one per batch at 0.003 per 1000 against 0.005 per 1000 for the PUT). Inline wrapped keys save a PUT, not a KMS request. M05.08 must measure the real KMS path before these envelopes are frozen.
- `latency-ha` pays about 9.8 MiB/s of cross-AZ bytes on busy load, an extra 0.5k USD per 30 days over the single-zone journal at the example 0.02/GB, plus the archive PUTs both journal profiles add.
- Skewed load is cheaper than busy (fewer, fuller batches on the hot tenant) under the fenced-append design, which has no head CAS. The measured hot-key amplification applies only to a payload-plus-head-CAS design with competing writers: about 1.29 PUT, 1.38 HEAD and 0.29 LIST per commit on that domain (M02.10). That is a further reason the head-CAS design is not the default.

## Recovery event (one cell loss)

Requests {'get': 66000, 'list': 1000, 'head': 0, 'put': 0, 'kms': 1000} (checkpoint and tail GETs, one bounded cold LIST per domain, KMS unwraps), about USD 0.03 per event. The tail read is 250 GiB at the upper bound; one process at the measured large-payload lower bound of 120 MiB/s needs at least 2133 s, so **the replay-tail bound, not request price, sets the RTO**: a tail bound, parallel readers or a smaller byte cap must come from M03/M06 measurement. upper-bound tail (every domain at its replay bound with full 4 MiB batches); one process; a smaller tail bound or parallel readers shortens it.

## Assumption checks

- Zero idle: an empty workload prices to USD 0 (no polling term exists in the model).
- Poisson/window: expected batches per domain per second for Poisson arrivals is rate / (1 + rate x wait); against the simulation:
  - sparse at 0.25 s: simulated 95.43, model 94.12, difference 1.4%
  - busy at 0.25 s: simulated 155.77, model 155.04, difference 0.5%
  - busy at 1.0 s: simulated 40.0, model 39.68, difference 0.8%
- Rejected: a price without source/date/unit, a negative or non-numeric price, an unpriced request kind (all tested).

## Constraints per profile (what is frozen, what is not)

| profile | frozen constraint (operator can change the inputs, not the semantics) | not established here |
|---|---|---|
| `object-economy` | max batch wait 1 s, one ordered lane per domain, one write process per tested rate, regional object commit | RTO (cell reactivation measured by M03/M07), KMS latency (M05.08) |
| `object-resilient` | max batch wait 250 ms, same commit path, N-1 serving capacity declared by the operator | surviving-capacity cost, cache duplication, read-path spend |
| `latency-ha` | three full voters in three zones, two cross-AZ copies, 5 s archive window as the RPO exposure bound | quorum fsync latency and journal operations (no prototype), archive lag under failure |
| `latency-zonal` | one-zone journal, 5 s archive window, explicit loss exposure on zone loss | same as above |

No universal USD budget or SLA is set. Constraints are expressed as measurable inputs (wait, bytes, copies, archive window) plus the priced components above for operator-supplied workloads and prices.
